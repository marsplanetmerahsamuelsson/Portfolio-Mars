Robotic Free-Form Timber

Master Thesis Project: Investigating Load-Bearing Free-Form Timber via Robotic Fabrication[cite: 6]

Project Overview

This thesis investigates the potential of manufacturing load-bearing free-form timber structures where timber elements can rotate freely along the X, Y, and Z axes[cite: 6]. The core of the research integrates material science—studying anisotropic timber behavior, viscoelasticity, and hygroscopic properties—with advanced 6-axis robotic fabrication and material preparation techniques[cite: 6].

Research & Fabrication Process

1. Material Setup & Softening

Solid timber lamellas (sized at 1,200 mm × 50 mm × 3 mm following Engineered Wood Products taxonomy) are prepared using specialized soaking and steam-bending tools[cite: 6]. Both water soaking and steam heating play major roles in softening the timber to ease the bending process[cite: 6].

2. Robotic Motion & Bending Experiments

The research evaluates five distinct experimental setups, exploring both direct 6-axis motion and inverse kinematic motion planning[cite: 6]:

  • Experiments 1–3: Explored single-layer and multi-layer timber bending using axis motions, demonstrating how increasing lamella layers helps the timber retain its new shape[cite: 6].
  • Experiments 4–5: Integrated inverse kinematic motion planning where tool reference points allow automated robot adjustments, successfully combining multiple lamella layers with steam-softening[cite: 6].

3. Robotic Assembly & Joinery

The workflow extends to robotic assembly, utilizing 3D-printed joints positioned via computed spatial planes to construct complex, non-planar geometries[cite: 6]. Because 3D-printed elements require additional support, the final assemblies incorporate traditional wood joinery, interlocking components, screws, and structural foundation bolts[cite: 6].

4. Architectural Application

The project concludes with a conceptual architectural application, demonstrating how robot-bent timber curvature combined with glulam principles can create efficient, beautiful, and sustainable structural spaces[cite: 6].
